Nine months ago I switched from Apple Watch to Garmin.

Garmin gave me much more running and training data, but I wanted the watch to feel more connected to me. Pretty quickly I had an idea: a little virtual version of myself living on the watch face.

Not a coach.

Not a training assistant.

A small companion that reflects my progress and gives me one more reason to keep showing up.

Mini Me.

I tried building it when I first got the watch, but the project got stuck.

The Garmin SDK had a learning curve, but the bigger blocker was design.

I was confident I could make the watch face work. I was much less confident I could make it feel like Mini Me.

Last week I gave vibe coding a real chance on a project I genuinely wanted to exist.

Three days later, I had a working Garmin watch face.

For this project, I shaped the task list, made product decisions, tested each step in the Garmin simulator, and manually checked the result on the physical watch.

This worked because the project was small enough: low risk, fast feedback, and easy to test on the actual device.

Mini Me is still small. Today it shows weekly running distance, daily steps, current weather, and sleepy mode when it's time to start getting ready for sleep.

I want Mini Me to help with consistency.

If he makes me happy enough to show up tomorrow, that is the win.

I'm signed up for a 45K race in December. After running the 32K Carmel-Kinneret Trail as a checkpoint, consistency became the thing I want to protect.

Mini Me is tiny, but if he gives me one extra reason to keep showing up, the project is already doing its job.
