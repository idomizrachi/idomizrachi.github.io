# Context

## Glossary

### Dev Blog

A personal engineering notebook for documenting logs, projects, ideas, and technical notes. Its primary audience is future Ido, with other engineers as a secondary audience.

### Reader

Someone browsing the dev blog. The primary reader is future Ido; secondary readers are other engineers who want to understand the work, decisions, and learning trail.

### Project

A durable development work stream or concept that may be revisited over time. A project can be a small tool, an app proof of concept, a complete project, or a development concept article, and placeholder projects are acceptable while the dev blog is taking shape; a project has a status of Planning, In Progress, Paused, Shipped, or Archived.

### Log

A dated content entry about progress, a decision, a lesson, or a technical note. Logs are the primary writing stream and may attach to at most one project or stand alone; a log may optionally have a type.
_Avoid_: Notes, Posts

### Article

A standalone content entry that explains or reflects on a development concept, practice, or tool. General articles are not tied to a specific project, though they may relate to recurring interests such as code generation, test-driven development, or local AI workflows.

### Vibe-Coded Project

A project where AI-assisted coding is a central part of moving from idea to working artifact, especially when the milestone is the changed development process as much as the shipped result.

### Agent-Directed Implementation

A development mode where Ido steers goals, tasks, and product decisions while an AI agent performs most of the coding work. In Mini Me, this included deliberately giving the agent near-total trust over implementation; it is distinct from traditional pair programming because code review may be minimal and the main human work is direction and acceptance.

### Acceptance-Driven Development

The Mini Me working mode where Ido judged agent output primarily by whether it compiled, ran in the Garmin simulator, and behaved correctly during manual tests on the physical watch. Code curiosity remained valuable, but acceptance replaced line-by-line authorship as the main feedback loop.

### Bootstrap Friction

The early project cost of turning a vague idea into a runnable, designed starting point. For Mini Me, Bootstrap Friction included the Garmin SDK learning curve and the design gap between Ido's programming ability and visual-design confidence.

### Mini Me

A personal Garmin watch face concept built around a small virtual version of Ido. Mini Me adds identity and emotional connection on top of Garmin's running and training data.
_Avoid_: Coach, training assistant

### Companion

A supportive presence that reflects progress and investment without prescribing workouts or judging performance. Mini Me is a companion: it grows as Ido's fitness improves and as he invests in himself.

### Consistency Memory

Mini Me's future ability to remember repeated effort over time, especially showing up regularly. Consistency Memory should reward patterns of care without punishing skipped days.
_Avoid_: Streak pressure, inactivity warning

### Positive Bias

The design rule that Mini Me should respond to effort, rest, recovery, and consistency with encouragement. Even low-energy states such as poor sleep or recovery should feel like reflection, not judgment.
_Avoid_: Shame, punishment, guilt

### Idea

A raw or lightweight concept that may or may not become a project. Ideas can have their own pages, but they are allowed to stay rough and exploratory; an idea may optionally have a status of Seed, Exploring, Promoted, or Archived.

### Page

A durable standalone section of the dev blog, such as About, Uses, Now, Stack, or Reading. Pages are not dated writing entries and are not part of the log stream.
